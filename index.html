Stage 1 · the sovereign laptop

Plant one seed. A whole office grows on your own machine.

The Seed Laptop is an office you own outright. One small signed file, the seed, grows documents, spreadsheets, slides, a calendar, notes and tasks, contacts and deals, double-entry accounts, a PDF writer, a knowledge notebook, an image editor and page layout, with Kar, the conductor, to find and open anything. It runs on the computer you already have, with no network at all. Files from the old world (PDF, Word, Excel, email, calendars, contacts, CSV, images, zip) are translated only at the edges. Everything you make is backed up to one encrypted vault that only your phrase opens.

Start here

Your first ten minutes

  1. Open the office. Open it here. The first visit stores the office in your browser; after that it opens with the network unplugged.
  2. Plant the stock seed. Type a phrase of at least 16 characters twice and press Plant the stock seed. The phrase is your key: it makes your owner identity and it is the only thing that opens your vault. Write it down and keep it somewhere safe. Nobody can recover it for you, by design.
  3. Meet Kar. Kar opens with its first receipt: what grew, from which seed, every block checked against its pin. Ask Kar help, list, open crm, find invoice or recent.
  4. Open a block from the left. Words, Sheets, Slides, Calendar, Notes and Tasks are in Office; contacts and deals in CRM; books in Accounts. Each block runs in its own sandbox with no network; what it saves stays in your office.
  5. Bring files in. In Files in, choose any PDF, DOCX, XLSX, CSV, EML, ICS, VCF, image or zip. Each file's bytes are checked before anything reads them, read in a sandbox, and kept unchanged. A damaged or disguised file is kept out and you are told why.
  6. Write out. In Write out, save a letter as PDF or Word, an email as EML for your own mail provider, an event as ICS, a table as CSV or Excel. Every file is checked on its way out: it carries nothing of your keys or internals.
  7. Save your vault. In Your vault, enter your phrase and save. Keep the vault file off this machine (a USB stick, your own cloud folder). On any new machine: open the office, choose Regrow, give the seed, the vault and your phrase, and everything comes back byte for byte.
  8. Add a device. In Your devices, pair a second machine by copying a code from one to the other and back. Both keep working offline and agree again when they meet; losing one loses nothing it had shared.
  9. Read your receipts. Every action leaves one sentence you can read in Receipts: what was done, to what, and what was checked.

On a machine with Node 25 you can also plant from the command line: node tools/plant.mjs --seed seeds/stock.full --home <folder>. It runs under Node's permission model with no network permission.

How it works

From one file to a working office

The edges: the only place old formats touch

The vault and regrowing

Saving your vault packs everything the office holds into one LIGHT stream, then encrypts it with AES-256-GCM under a key stretched from your phrase (PBKDF2-SHA256, 600,000 rounds). The vault's header is authenticated with the body, so one changed bit anywhere refuses the whole file: a damaged vault restores nothing, never something else. To regrow, a fresh machine needs three things: the seed, the vault and your phrase. No server, no account, no estate service. The regrown machine makes a key of its own, and the behaviour watch (BES) starts on it in observe-only, refusing nothing while it learns how you work, so you can never be locked out of your own office.

Your devices

Every device has its own random key, and your phrase signs the roster of devices allowed to sync. A change travels as a signed packet that the receiving device checks at its door before reading a byte of it; the relay between them reads no body. Two devices edited apart agree as soon as they meet, and two edits to the same thing are both kept (one as a conflict copy), never silently dropped. In a browser, two devices join directly over WebRTC with a pairing code you copy from one to the other and back; no outside server is involved.

Security, compliance, data protection

Private by construction

Nothing leaves

Measured three ways over a sealed day of more than 300 actions: from below the network on GitHub's runner (a packet capture on the host side of a locked namespace), with every network call trapped on a laptop, and in a Chrome tab. The only traffic allowed is the two emails the user chooses to send, to their own provider; claims L6, L6b and L6c2 in Results give the counts.

The software is what you approved

The seed is signed by its publisher; every block file is pinned by SHA-256. One changed bit in the genome, or in any block, and nothing grows. A light seed fetches blocks from your own mirror and checks every one the same way.

Hostile files stay out

Every incoming file is checked on its raw bytes (type, size, magic, shape) before any reader sees it, and read only in a sandbox with no network and capped memory and time. A file glued to another, padded past its cap, whose bytes are another kind than its name, or carrying an Office macro is kept out; a cut-short file is kept out wherever its format can tell (PDF, Word, Excel, zip, images, multipart mail, calendars, cards). A file kept out once is refused on sight after that. What can still come in is a damaged file the format itself cannot tell from a good one (a flipped bit in plain text or in an image's pixels): it is read only in the sandbox, never by the office's core. Claim L2b counts all of it.

Keys stay with the owner

Your identity comes from your phrase; each device has its own key; your vault opens only with your phrase. The estate holds nothing of yours, so there is nothing of yours to breach.

An audit trail by default

Every action leaves a receipt in plain words, kept in the store and the vault: what was done, to what, and what was checked.

GDPR by design

No server, no processor, no transfer: personal data is processed only on the controller's own devices, encrypted at rest in the vault, and exportable in standard formats at any time.

Risks, answered straight

What to know before you plant

If someone forgets their phrase, is the vault gone?

Yes. The phrase is the only key, by design, so no one (including us) can open a vault without it. That is what keeps it private. Keep the phrase written down somewhere safe, or in your company's password manager; the office never locks out a person who has it.

What does it not replace?

Email hosting and video calls, bank feeds, filing to the tax office, e-signing other people's PDFs, vector drawing and team design libraries. The cost case marks each of these and counts none of them as saved.

Is it a new operating system?

No. Stage 1 runs on the operating system already there. A minimal base image is defined for clean machines; the office itself is the same either way.

Do files come back exactly?

Structured formats (Word, Excel, email, calendars, contacts, CSV, images, zip) are measured field by field; PDF text by word. The table in Results says which formats were exact in the measured run and which are best effort, from the run's own numbers.

What failed?

Every sealed claim that did not hold is shown in Results as FAILED, with what was measured and why. Failures are kept, never re-run until they pass. Three failed because of a fault in the measuring instrument; each has a fix claim sealed before its own run. Two are about the office itself: damaged files that carry nothing a format can check are still read (in the sandbox), and the tab day left more receipts than actions.

White paper

The seed laptop, explained

1. The idea

A computer's useful life is the software and the data on it, and most of both is now rented: an office suite per seat, a CRM per seat, accounts per company, storage per terabyte, each with its own copy of your data. The seed laptop turns that around. One signed file names the blocks of an office and pins each by its hash; planting it grows the whole office locally, offline, in seconds; one encrypted vault holds everything made since. The machine stops mattering: any machine with a browser, the seed, the vault and the phrase is the same office.

2. The seed

The genome is canonical JSON, signed with Ed25519 by its publisher: the blocks to grow, the SHA-256 of every file in each, the office's settings. The full seed packs the genome and every block's files into one LIGHT vault (the stock seed: seven blocks, eight files, 798 KB as files, 101 KB packed). Germination checks the signature, then every pin, then composes the blocks into the office, makes the owner's identity from the phrase, and writes Kar's first receipt. A change to any bit of the genome, or to any block file, refuses the seed.

3. The office

Each block is existing estate gear, unchanged: a single-page app that already worked on its own. It runs in a sandboxed frame served by the office's own service worker, with a content policy that allows it no network at all. A small bridge, loaded before the block's own code, gives it local storage and an IndexedDB that write into one shared store, a multi-value CRDT in which concurrent edits are both kept and nothing is lost silently. The block never knows it is part of an office.

4. The edges

Old formats are translated only at the edges. Incoming bytes are checked before any parsing: the declared type, a size cap per type, the format's magic bytes, and its shape at the end (a PDF's %%EOF, a zip's end record, an image's trailer), which is what catches a file glued to another. Only then does a reader decode it, and only in a sandbox: on a laptop a separate Node process under the permission model (no network, no writes, no child processes, reads limited to the reader's own files, a 64 MB heap and a 5 s limit); in a browser a sandboxed frame. The readers check what each format can check about itself (every zip CRC, every PNG chunk CRC, JPEG, GIF and WebP structure, every PDF stream, a multipart message's closing boundary, a calendar's and a card's END). A file that fails is quarantined with its reason and remembered, so the same bytes are refused on sight next time (only refusals the bytes alone decide are remembered, and only under the same version of the edge, so there is never a false immunity). Files going out are written in plain standard form with no producer, author or application metadata, then scanned for anything of the office's internals: keys, the vault reference, block hashes, the product's names, the home folder, the user's name.

5. The vault

The vault is a LIGHT stream of the store, encrypted with AES-256-GCM under a key from the owner's phrase (PBKDF2-SHA256, 600,000 rounds); its 37-byte header is the cipher's additional data. Regrowing on a fresh machine needs only the seed, the vault and the phrase. The regrown device makes its own random key (it must never reuse the old device's write counters), and the behaviour watch starts in observe-only: BES, the behavioural entropy layer after Gary W. Floyd's method, learns the owner's habits before it may refuse anything, so the documented recovery path is never blocked by it.

6. Devices

The owner's phrase signs a roster of devices. Every change is a signed LIGHT packet, refused at the receiving door unless it comes from a device on the roster, is correctly signed, is newer than the last from that device, and is addressed to the receiver. The relay that carries packets between devices reads no body. In a browser, devices pair directly over a WebRTC data channel, with the connection details exchanged by hand as a code; no STUN or signalling server is used.

7. Proof

Every claim on this page was written down before the code that measures it, and its instruments, data and bounds were fixed before the first measured run. Each claim was then measured once. The receipts name the commit and the machine. A claim that fails is reported as failed, with its numbers, and kept.

8. Honest scope

Stage 1 is the sovereign laptop on the operating system already there: "no operating system" is not a Stage 1 claim, and bare metal is a research path only. The minimal base is a reproducible image definition. The readers are JavaScript in a sandbox, not WebAssembly. Mail goes out through the owner's own provider and comes in as message files; the owner connects their own accounts. Stages 2 (one organism across devices, interfaces grown on demand) and 3 (a living extension that learns, works ahead, defends itself and federates) come only after Stage 1 is proven, each as its own sealed organ.

FAQ

Questions

Does it need the internet?

Only to fetch the office page the first time, or a light seed's blocks from your mirror. After that, nothing it does needs a network, and nothing it does sends anything, except an email you choose to send through your own provider.

Where is my data?

In your browser's storage on your machine, and in the vault file you save, wherever you keep it. Nowhere else.

Can I bring my old files?

Yes: PDF, Word, Excel, CSV, email (EML), calendars (ICS), contacts (VCF), PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP and zip. The original is always kept unchanged next to its reading.

Can my files go back out?

Yes: PDF, Word, Excel, CSV, email and calendar files, in plain standard form. Claim L3 opens every one in mainstream readers (LibreOffice, poppler, PyMuPDF, pypdf, python-docx, openpyxl, Python's email, icalendar and csv) and checks the content comes back.

Why are macros refused?

An Office macro is a program inside a document. The office never runs one and never reads a file that carries one: it is refused as a rule, whatever the macro holds.

What is Kar?

The conductor: it lists, opens and finds anything in your office, shows what was done most recently, and writes the receipts. It works with no AI model at all; if you add a local one, it can only choose from what Kar already does.
